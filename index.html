About

Two threads: code and aircraft.

I wrote my first lines of Python at twelve and felt something click. That curiosity turned into game jams on itch.io — GMTK, Brackeys, a NOKIA 3310 jam — where I learned to ship small Unity projects on a deadline and debug my way out of trouble. Somewhere along the way, coding stopped feeling like a skill and started feeling like a language I actually spoke.

Around the same time, I became fascinated by aircraft — military aircraft in particular — and the engineering packed into machines that move through the sky. My grandfather works at the intersection of software and hardware in aviation, and his stories turned that fascination into a direction. Computer Engineering is where those two threads meet, and it's exactly what I'm headed to Montana State University to study.

In high school I spent two years in the Cisco Networking Academy, racking real equipment, writing full lab reports, and earning my CCNA before senior year. Through the Cisco Project Club, I helped support classroom tech and donate IT equipment to communities in Antigua. I added the AWS Cloud Practitioner certification in May 2026, and I've run the A/V and livestream setup at my church since 2021.

Long term, I'm aiming at the layer where engineering decisions have physical consequences: avionics, embedded systems, and flight software. When I'm not at a keyboard, I'm usually behind a camera at an airfield.
